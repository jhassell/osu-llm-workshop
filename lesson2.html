2.1 Deriving the Equation

Start with the equilibrium expression for a weak acid HA:

Kₐ = [H⁺][A⁻] / [HA]

Take the negative log of both sides:

–log Kₐ = –log[H⁺] – log([A⁻]/[HA])

pKₐ = pH – log([A⁻]/[HA])

Rearrange to get the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation:

pH = pKₐ + log([A⁻] / [HA])

What This Means

  • Buffer pH depends on the ratio of conjugate base to weak acid, not their absolute concentrations
  • When [A⁻] = [HA], the log term is 0, so pH = pKₐ
  • Increasing [A⁻]/[HA] raises pH; decreasing it lowers pH

2.2 Using the Equation: Calculating Buffer pH

Example: Acetate Buffer

Calculate the pH of a buffer containing 0.15 M CH₃COO⁻ and 0.10 M CH₃COOH (pKₐ = 4.76).

pH = 4.76 + log(0.15 / 0.10)

pH = 4.76 + log(1.5)

pH = 4.76 + 0.18 = 4.94

Example: Phosphate Buffer

A buffer is made from 0.050 M H₂PO₄⁻ and 0.10 M HPO₄²⁻ (pKₐ = 7.21). What is its pH?

pH = 7.21 + log(0.10 / 0.050)

pH = 7.21 + log(2.0)

pH = 7.21 + 0.30 = 7.51

2.3 Preparing a Buffer of Target pH

To prepare a buffer at a specific pH, rearrange the equation to find the needed ratio:

[A⁻] / [HA] = 10^(pH – pKₐ)

Example: Prepare pH 5.00 Acetate Buffer

Target pH = 5.00, pKₐ = 4.76

[A⁻]/[HA] = 10^(5.00 – 4.76) = 10^0.24 = 1.74

So you need about 1.74 times more acetate than acetic acid. For a 0.20 M total buffer:

[HA] + [A⁻] = 0.20 M

[A⁻] = 1.74[HA]

2.74[HA] = 0.20 M → [HA] = 0.073 M, [A⁻] = 0.127 M

Choosing the Right Buffer System

Pick a weak acid with pKₐ within ±1 unit of your target pH. This ensures both components are present in reasonable amounts (ratio between 0.1 and 10).

2.4 Dilution & the Henderson–Hasselbalch Equation

A beautiful property: diluting a buffer does not change its pH (ideally).

Since pH depends on the ratio [A⁻]/[HA], and both concentrations decrease by the same factor on dilution, the ratio stays constant. The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation predicts no pH change.

In reality, very dilute buffers (< 0.01 M) deviate due to activity effects and water autoionization — but for typical lab concentrations, dilution barely changes pH.
