# Bill depth against bill length in the Palmer penguins

Generated by analysis.py from penguins_raw.csv. Every number below is computed by
the script; rerun it to rebuild this file and the three figures.

## The data

The raw file has 344 rows (one adult penguin each) and 17 columns. Birds per
species: Adelie 152, Chinstrap 68, Gentoo 124.
Two birds have no body measurements at all (N2A2, N38A2), so
2 rows are dropped and 342 are used. Sex is missing for 11 birds
and 36 birds come from nests never seen with a full clutch; neither matters
for this question, so those birds stay in.

## What the plots show

Pooled across species, deeper bills go with shorter bills: r = -0.24, slope
-0.085 mm of depth per mm of length (figure 1). Within every species the
relationship is positive (figure 2):

| Species | n | Mean length (mm) | Mean depth (mm) | r | Slope (mm/mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adelie | 151 | 38.8 | 18.3 | 0.39 | 0.179 |
| Chinstrap | 68 | 48.8 | 18.4 | 0.65 | 0.222 |
| Gentoo | 123 | 47.5 | 15.0 | 0.64 | 0.205 |

The reversal happens because species differ in both measurements at once: Gentoo bills
are long and shallow, Adelie bills short and deep (figure 3, where each species sits against the dashed pooled line). Species is the confounder.
Pooling hides it and reports a negative relationship that holds for no species.
