# Oklahoma county winter wheat yield, 2000 to 2025

Generated by analysis.py from ok_county_winter_wheat_2000-2025.csv. Every number below is
computed by the script; rerun it to rebuild this file and the three figures.

## The data

The raw file is long: 7156 rows, each one statistic (yield, acres planted, acres
harvested or production) for one county and year. Values are text with thousands
separators. There are no county estimates for 2024. Dropped:
1284 rows that split a county into irrigated and non-irrigated (published only to
2009; the all-practices rows already cover them) and 688 rows with county
code 998 ("OTHER (COMBINED) COUNTIES" within a district, "OTHER COUNTIES" statewide from
2020), which pool counties NASS did not publish separately. 336 rows are both, so 1636
rows are dropped in all, not the sum of the two. After reshaping, 1380 county-years have a yield, from 75 counties;
the number of counties published per year ranges from 38 to 75, and fewer are published after 2007.
As a consistency check, production divided by harvested acres reproduces the published
yield to within its rounding (0.1 bushel, or a whole bushel where NASS published whole
bushels) for 1344 of 1380 county-years.

## What the plots show

County yields swing far more from year to year than they trend (figure 1). Production
over harvested acres for the published counties was highest in 2020
(40.1 bu per acre) and lowest in 2014
(16.6). The 2000 to 2004 average was 33.8
and the 2021 to 2025 average 33.1 (figure 2), but these average different sets of
published counties (71 to 75 then, 40 to 53 now), so they
are not a trend; extension.md compares each county with itself. Districts move together in the
bad years (figure 3), which is what statewide weather does. On average only 66% of
planted acres were harvested for grain; much Oklahoma wheat is grazed by cattle or
abandoned.
