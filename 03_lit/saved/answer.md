**Key caveat:** The Tautges 2019 study shows that measuring only the top 30 cm gives a false positive (+1.44 Mg C ha⁻¹) when the full 2 m profile shows a net loss (–13.4 Mg C ha⁻¹). Depth of measurement critically changes the sign of the effect.

## Explanation for Why Values Differ

### 1. Sampling depth is the dominant factor
- **Shallow sampling (0–15/0–30 cm)** consistently shows gains: Chahal +3.7 to +18.3 Mg C ha⁻¹ (0–15 cm); Tautges +1.44 Mg C ha⁻¹ (0–30 cm).
- **Deep sampling (30–200 cm)** reveals losses: Tautges –14.86 Mg C ha⁻¹ (30–200 cm).
- **Full-profile sampling (0–200 cm)** shows net loss: Tautges –13.4 Mg C ha⁻¹.
- Gentsch sampled to 40 cm and found **no significant difference** between CC and fallow (row 4), consistent with Tautges: gains in topsoil may be offset by subsoil losses.

### 2. Duration and legacy effects
- Chahal (9 yr): accumulation rate 1.1–2.2 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹.
- Poeplau & Don meta-analysis (cited in Chahal): 0.32 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ (0–22 cm). The meta-analysis includes shorter and longer studies; the lower rate may reflect saturation over time.
- Gentsch (6 yr): no detectable stock difference despite concentration increases (rows 1–2). Stock changes lag concentration changes.

### 3. What the baseline is
- Chahal: CC vs. **bare fallow** (no cover crop).
- Gentsch: CC vs. **fallow** (mechanical/herbicide maintenance).
- Tautges: WCC vs. **conventional management** (which may include other C inputs).
- Zheng: cover crop vs. **bare fallow (CK)**, and **+chicken grazing vs. cover crop alone** (5.2% increase, row 15).
- Different baselines produce different effect sizes.

### 4. Climate, soil type, and cropping system
- Chahal: temperate humid, sandy loam, vegetable/grain rotation (Ontario).
- Gentsch: temperate oceanic, silty loam, wheat–maize rotation (Germany).
- Zheng: subtropical, tidal clay, double rice with winter cover crop + chicken grazing (China).
- Tautges: Mediterranean, maize–tomato rotation, 2 m profile (California).
- These differences in climate, texture, and rotation explain part of the spread.

### 5. Units and metrics not comparable
- Gentsch reports **concentration (%)** (rows 1–2) and **stock (t ha⁻¹)** (row 3). Concentration increased over time in all treatments including fallow, but stocks did not differ.
- Zheng reports **concentration (g kg⁻¹)** (rows 17–25) and **relative % change** (row 15). Without bulk density, these cannot be converted to Mg C ha⁻¹.
- Chahal and Tautges report **stock (Mg C ha⁻¹)** directly comparable.

## Hypotheses Tested and Rejected

| Hypothesis | Test | Result |
|------------|------|--------|
| "Cover crops always increase SOC stock" | Compare Tautges 0–30 cm (+1.44) vs 0–200 cm (–13.4) | **Rejected**: sign flips with depth. |
| "Effect size is consistent across climates" | Chahal (Canada) 4.4–18.3 vs Gentsch (Germany) no sig. diff. | **Rejected**: Gentsch found no stock difference despite 6 yr. |
| "Meta-analysis rate (0.32) applies to all studies" | Chahal rate 1.1–2.2 is 3–7× higher | **Rejected**: site-specific factors dominate. |
| "Concentration increase = stock increase" | Gentsch: concentration rose in all treatments, but stocks did not differ | **Rejected**: bulk density changes can decouple them. |

## What Remains Unexplained

1. **Subsoil carbon dynamics**: Tautges found losses at 30–200 cm with winter cover crops alone. Is this a priming effect, reduced root C at depth, or redistribution? The three papers only sampled to 40 cm max (Gentsch) or 20 cm (Zheng).

2. **Mechanism of C gain vs. loss mitigation**: Chahal could not determine if CCs mitigate C losses or intensify stabilization (p. 6). Gentsch found concentration increases in fallow too, suggesting other drivers.

3. **Species/mixture effects within studies**: Chahal shows OSR + Rye and OSR outperforming oat and cereal rye (rows 11–12). Gentsch shows mixtures storing more OC in large aggregates (16–8 mm) while singles store in <1 mm. But neither links this to net stock changes at depth.

4. **Bulk density co-variation**: Gentsch reports strong negative OC–BD correlation (R²=0.44). Without BD, Zheng's concentrations (12–15 g kg⁻¹) cannot be converted to stocks. This is a systematic gap.

5. **Long-term saturation**: Poeplau & Don's meta-analysis rate (0.32 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹) is much lower than Chahal's 1.1–2.2. Is Chahal's site early in the saturation curve? The 19-yr Tautges study shows net loss at depth, suggesting possible redistribution rather than net sequestration.

## Conclusion

Cover crops can increase surface SOC (0–15/0–30 cm) by **1–20 Mg C ha⁻¹** over 6–19 years, with accumulation rates of **0.3–2.2 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹**. However, **full-profile accounting may reverse the sign** (Tautges: –13.4 Mg C ha⁻¹ to 2 m). Sampling depth, baseline comparison, climate, and duration explain most of the spread. The three papers plus Tautges 2019 cannot resolve subsoil mechanisms or long-term saturation; deeper, longer experiments with bulk density are needed.
