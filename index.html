Buffer pH: A Three-Lesson Introduction

For first-year chemistry students

Welcome

Buffers are solutions that resist pH changes when small amounts of acid or base are added. They are essential in biological systems, laboratory work, and industrial processes. This workshop walks you through three short lessons:

Lesson 1: What Is a Buffer?

Components, mechanism, and why buffers resist pH change

Lesson 2: The Henderson–Hasselbalch Equation

Calculating buffer pH and preparing buffers of a target pH

Lesson 3: Buffer Capacity & Applications

Limits of buffers, biological examples, and lab practice

Before You Start

  • Acid–base definitions (Brønsted–Lowry)
  • Weak acids, weak bases, and conjugate pairs
  • Equilibrium constants (Ka, Kb) and pKa
  • Logarithms and pH = –log[H+]

If any of these feel shaky, review them first — they're the foundation for everything here.
